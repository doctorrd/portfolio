"Where are high-potential candidates plentiful, and why aren't they applying?"

From regional potential to the next postal area to activate

I built a geographic opportunity model that moved from regional potential down to postal-area priorities. It sized a 10,000+ person local audience opportunity and flagged the underperforming areas for recruitment focus.

RoleDeveloped the analytical story, region to local
MethodQuantify-and-locate; lookalike vs applicant comparison

The challenge

Potential everywhere, applications somewhere

The organization needed to know where lookalikes of its priority audience were plentiful but applications were not arriving at a comparable rate. Recruitment resources are limited, so a province-level answer was too broad to act on.

My role

Author of the region-to-local story

I developed the analytical story from broad geography down to local activation, compared applicant and successful-applicant patterns, refined the slide logic, and turned the findings into a prioritized recruitment-opportunity narrative. This sat within a wider program that also delivered 16 persona profiles across two language markets.

Approach

How it was done

  1. Quantify

    Size high-potential lookalike populations for the priority target group.

  2. Locate

    Compare lookalikes with actual applicants at provincial, municipal and postal level.

  3. Drill into one city

    Focus on a single high-potential market to make the opportunity concrete.

  4. Compare three postal areas

    Separate areas with healthy applicant traction from those with weaker activity.

  5. Keep potential separate from outcomes

    Present opportunity sizes as potential, never as recruitment results.

Outcome

The value created

High-level audience potential became specific locations where outreach and conversion efforts can be tested. Potential population is kept clearly separate from realized applicant outcomes, which makes the story easy for non-technical audiences to follow and to act on.
